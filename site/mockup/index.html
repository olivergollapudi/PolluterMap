How we estimate this

All numbers are estimates for one year of each facility's pollution (2023), in 2024 dollars, built only from public government data and published methods.

  • Deaths from air pollution: each facility's soot and smog-forming emissions (EPA National Emissions Inventory) × EPA's estimates of premature deaths per ton for that industry in Indiana.
  • Deaths from climate change: its greenhouse gases (EPA Greenhouse Gas Reporting Program) × the "mortality cost of carbon" (Bressler 2021): about one heat-related death worldwide, by 2100, per 4,434 tons of CO2.
  • Damage in dollars: health damage (EPA's value of a statistical life, $11.5 million, × air-pollution deaths) plus climate damage (EPA's 2023 social cost of each greenhouse gas).
  • Cost to offset: what it would cost to pull the same CO2 back out of the air with direct air capture (IPCC: $100–300 a ton at scale; today it costs more).
  • Illnesses: EPA's per-ton estimates of lung cancers, heart attacks, strokes, asthma, ER visits and hospital stays.

Not yet counted: toxic chemicals (cancer risk), lead, and water and land pollution. There's no accepted public method to turn those into deaths or dollars per facility, so they're shown as facts, not estimates. Medical costs (hospital bills) are coming next.
